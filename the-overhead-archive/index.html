Vol. I — A Field Record of Ordinary Skies

The Overhead
Archive

A photo journal by Utsab Das

Every day, without asking, the sky rearranges itself overhead — and almost no one looks up long enough to notice. This archive is an attempt to look up anyway: nine plates, shot between one ordinary sunrise and the next, kept in the order they were seen.

The sequence borrows its voice from Percy Bysshe Shelley's The Cloud — not to illustrate the poem, but to stand beside it, two records of the same restless thing.

From the Book

From The Cloud

I wield the flail of the lashing hail,
And bind the Sun's throne with a burning zone.
I bring fresh showers for the thirsting flowers,
And then again I dissolve it in rain.
Sublime on the towers of my skiey bowers,
From my wings are shaken the dews that waken.
The sanguine Sunrise leaps on the back of my sailing rack,
As on the jag of a mountain crag.
That orbed maiden with white fire laden,
Whom mortals call the Moon,
Glides glimmering o'er my fleece-like floor,
By the midnight breezes strewn.

By Percy Bysshe Shelley

About the Archive

The Overhead Archive

A photo journal by Utsab Das, gathering nine photographs of the sky taken in passing — over rooftops, roads, and temple spires — and arranging them alongside Percy Bysshe Shelley's The Cloud.

The pairing is not illustration but companionship: a nineteenth-century voice for the cloud, and a twenty-first-century record of the same sky, still doing what it has always done.

U.D.
